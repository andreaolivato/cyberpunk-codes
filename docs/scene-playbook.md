# Scene playbook: conversations, choices and holocalls

Everything needed to author a `.scene` for a mod, and to put a holocall on
screen. Established 2026-08-11 building gig 01's Batch C. **Read this before
touching anything under `source/wkit/raw/mod/*/scenes/` or `tools/gen_scenes.py`.**

Reference material this was derived from, all re-extractable:

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| A vanilla gig-briefing holocall | `basegame_4_gamedata.archive` → `base\open_world\minor_activities\santo_domingo\arroyo\ma_std_arr_03\` (`.questphase` + `scenes\*.scene`) |
| A mod scene with choices | `OneMoreLight.archive` → `base\quest\deceptious_quests\nq_1_more_light.scene` |
| A mod scene with spawned actors | `Californication.archive` → `base\quest\deceptious_quests\californication.scene` |

Extract with `WolvenKit.CLI unbundle <archive> -o <dir> --regex ...`, then
`WolvenKit.CLI convert serialize <dir>` to get readable JSON.

## Why bother

Dialogue choices exist nowhere else. `UIInteractions.DialogChoiceHubs` is
written natively and the confirm press is dispatched back to the scene that owns
the hub, so a script-injected hub has no owner to route the selection to. If the
player has to pick a line, it is a scene.

**There is a second, stronger reason: AUDIO.** A scene line carries a
`scnlocLocstringId` and the game resolves both its subtitle and its voiceover
from that one RUID. A scripted subtitle has no RUID, so **nothing can ever play
audio for it**. If anyone has to HEAR the line, it is a scene too. That route is
documented as a dead end in `architecture.md`.

## Anatomy of a `.scene`

`scnSceneResource`, generated by `tools/gen_scenes.py`:

- **`actors[]` / `playerActors[]`**: who can speak. They share ONE actorId
  space even though they are two arrays (Californication numbers its player
  actor 3, after scene actors 0-2), and `scnscreenplayDialogLine.speaker` is a
  bare `scnActorId` with no discriminator.
- **`sceneGraph.graph[]`**: `scnStartNode`, `scnSectionNode`, `scnChoiceNode`,
  `scnHubNode`, `scnEndNode` (and `scnQuestNode`, which embeds a real quest node
. We do not use it, see "Where to put the logic" below).
- **`screenplayStore.lines[]` / `.options[]`**: one entry per spoken line and
  per choice, each pointing at a locstring id.
- **`locStore`**: the text itself, embedded in the scene file, per locale.
  Scene lines do NOT resolve through our ArchiveXL onscreens resource. This is
  the one place in the repo where strings are not LocKeys.
- **`entryPoints` / `exitPoints`**: named nodes. These names become the input
  and output socket names of the quest phase's scene node.

### Sockets

Every node has `outputSockets`, each with a `stamp {name, ordinal}` and a list
of destinations carrying an `isockStamp {name, ordinal}`.

- Plain flow is output `(0,0)` → destination input `(0,0)`.
- A section additionally carries an empty socket named 1, and one extra
  socket per `scneventsSocket` event (name 2+, fired at a given time inside the
  section: that is how vanilla kicks off a quest node mid-line).
- A choice uses output `(0,i)` for option *i*, in the same order as its
  `options[]`, then six empty sockets named 1-6.

Match the shipped shape. It is cheaper than discovering which sockets the engine
indexes positionally.

### Timing

`scnDialogLineEvent.duration` and `startTime` are milliseconds inside the
section, and `sectionDuration` is the total.

**Unvoiced:** these numbers are the only thing pacing the conversation, and
`gen_scenes.py` estimates them from text length (~55 ms/char).

**Voiced:** they must come from the real clip or lines cut off or drag.
`gen_voice.py` measures every WAV into `durations.json` and `gen_scenes.py`
prefers it over the estimate, so the two halves coexist without a flag. **Run
gen_voice BEFORE gen_scenes**. The other way round paces the scene to the
previous run's audio. A gendered line is paced from the LONGER of the two takes,
so it finishes whichever V the player is.

Still true either way: no soundbank and no lipsync anim set are required
(`lipsyncAnimSet.id = 4294967295`).

## Holocalls

The chrome (ring, avatar, name, "Connection 541.44.10") and the dialogue are
separate systems that meet at a fact.

**The dialogue** is an ordinary scene whose caller lines set
`scnDialogLineVoParams.isHolocallSpeaker = 1` and
`voExpression = Vo_Expression_Phone`. That flag is what routes a line into the
call instead of the world. (There is no `Vo_Expression_Normal`; the enum starts
at `Vo_Expression_Spoken`).

**The chrome** is `PhoneSystem`, driven by `questTriggerCallRequest`
(`cyberpunk/systems/phoneSystem.swift:79`):

```
caller / addressee   CNames. The non-player one must be a journal contact's ID.
callPhase            IncomingCall -> StartCall -> EndCall
callMode             Video = holocall, Audio = voice only
isRejectable, showAvatar, visuals
```

Vanilla queues these from the quest graph, through a per-contact holocall phase
that the scene talks to with `holo_<contact>_calls_v_start_activate` /
`..._start_done` facts.

**CORRECTED 2026-08-12.** This used to read "a mod has no such phase for its own
contact" and was taken to mean mods cannot use them at all. Separate the two:

- **True for a contact we invent.** Elena is merged in by ArchiveXL, no vanilla
  phase exists for her, and `Gig01_Holocall.reds` is the only way to ring V.
- **False for any BASE-GAME contact.** `base\quest\holocalls\<contact>\` ships a
  full `_holocall.questphase` + `_holocall.scene` for ~60 of them, `nix`,
  `mama_welles`, `regina`... They sit in the base quest graph and fire on
  `holo_<contact>_calls_v_start_activate`. `nix_holocall.scene` even activates
  the `nix_holo` spawn set and issues a real Video holocall itself, which the
  scripted route cannot do.

So for a base-game contact, setting one fact is likely cheaper AND better than
driving the chrome from script. See `docs/backlog.md` 3d. The mistake was
generalising from the only contact that had been tried.

`Gig01_Holocall.reds` plays that part from script for OUR contacts. The
handshake:

1. quest phase sets a fact → script queues `IncomingCall`, phone rings
2. player answers → the game writes
   `phonecall_<caller>_with_<addressee>` (both lowercased - 
   `PhoneSystem.GetPhoneCallFactName`) with a `questPhoneTalkingState`:
   **Ended 0, Initializing 1, Talking 2, Rejected 3**
3. on `Talking` the script queues `StartCall`. **Answering alone does not turn
   the chrome into a holocall**. Only the `StartCall` phase does, and sets the
   fact the quest phase waits on before entering the scene
4. the scene ends → quest phase sets a fact → script queues `EndCall`

### Audio, not Video: a script-issued Video holocall CRASHES the game

Found the hard way on 2026-08-11. The game died the instant the player
answered, with nothing in any log but a `CrashInfo.json`.

`questPhoneCallMode.Video` means the phone draws a live render of the caller
into `HudPhoneAvatarController.m_HolocallRenderTexture`, and vanilla stages a
body for that render to point at. `base\cinematics\scene_blocks\
sb_holocall_initializer.scene` does it with `questCallContact_NodeType`, which
carries a field the scripted route does not have:

```
prefabNodeRef: "#holocalls_studio"      mode: Video      phase: StartCall
```

**`questTriggerCallRequest` has no `prefabNodeRef`.** There is no way to point a
script-issued Video call at a holocall studio, so it renders a caller that was
never staged. `Audio` has no render texture at all, `RefreshView` only shows it
for `EHudAvatarMode.Holocall`: which is why the scripted route is safe in Audio
and not in Video. `showAvatar` still puts the contact's portrait on screen.

So: from script, a call is Audio.

**A true video holocall is CLOSED, tried and ruled out** (2026-08-13). The
only route is to let vanilla's per-contact holocall phase own the call, and that
hands over the whole call, including that contact's own small-talk dialogue
options, precisely the clutter already rejected. Video and vanilla's
options are the same switch; there is no version that takes one and leaves the
other. `backlog.md` 3d, and the code for it has been removed.

Related, and CORRECTED 2026-08-13. The vanilla scene this was modelled on, the
NCPD dispatcher brief, has no player actor at all. This playbook concluded that
ours should not either, "so nothing asks the scene system to stage the player at
a marker he may be a kilometre away from."

**That inference was wrong and it was expensive.** `scnPlayerActorDef` has no
spawn, marker, offset or position field of any kind; it cannot stage the player,
it can only find him. Believing otherwise meant V had no spoken line anywhere,
which forced all of his dialogue into choice hubs and made this gig's hub density
four times vanilla's. See "V speaks: the player actor" above.

**A mod-added contact works as an addressee.** `HudPhoneGameController`
`GetIncomingContact` walks `JournalManager.GetContacts()` and matches
`JournalContact.GetId()`, so an ArchiveXL-merged contact behaves exactly like a
base-game one: that is also where the avatar and the displayed name come from.
The id is the last path segment: `contacts/elena_ortega` → `n"elena_ortega"`.

**The phone rings for 8 seconds and then times out**
(`HudPhoneGameController.m_TimeoutPeroid` → `PhoneTimeoutRequest` → `EndCall`).
An unanswered call is normal, not an error. Ring again, or the gig dead-ends on
a missed call. Also check `PhoneSystem.IsCallingEnabled()` before ringing, in
combat or mid-menu the call is dropped on the floor.

## V speaks: the player actor, and the warning that was wrong

**`scnPlayerActorDef` cannot move the player.** Round-trip any vanilla scene that
has one (`sts_hey_gle_04_johnny.scene`) and read the whole class: acquisitionPlan,
actorId, the anim-set arrays, findActorInContextParams, findNetworkPlayerParams,
lipsyncAnimSet, playerName, specAppearance, specCharacterRecordId, specTemplate,
voicetagId. There is no spawn, marker, offset or position field of any kind - 
unlike `scnActorDef`, which carries `spawnDespawnParams` with a `spawnMarker` and
a `spawnOffset`. `findInContext` finds V wherever he is standing.

So a player actor is safe in a holocall that fires while V is across the city,
and it is safe in a scene whose marker is a kilometre away. This corrects an
earlier note here that said the opposite; that note was inference, and it cost
this gig every spoken V line .

Two consequences:

- **A player line is audible by construction.** It plays from V's own position,
  which is the listener's position. The world-emitter problem that silences a
  voice-only actor a kilometre away cannot arise.
- **The failure mode is benign.** If a player line does not resolve audio, it is
  a silent subtitle, which is the status quo, not a regression. Nothing about
  the scene stalls.

V's lines are ordinary sections: `visualStyle: regular`,
`voExpression: Vo_Expression_Spoken`, `isHolocallSpeaker: 0` **even inside a
holocall**. He is the one holding the phone, not the voice coming out of it.
`voicetagId` is vanilla's `1103967280742240864`; `lipsyncAnimSet.id` stays
4294967295 here because our `resouresReferences.lipsyncAnimSets` is empty.

### Staging a scene AT THE PLAYER: `scnWorldMarker.type = "Tag"`

`sceneLocation` does not have to be a fixed NodeRef. `scnWorldMarker` has a
`type` field, and across 358 shipped street-story questphases it takes two
values:

| type | uses | shape |
|---|---|---|
| `NodeRef` | 929 | `nodeRef: "#some_node"`, `tag: None` |
| **`Tag`** | 73 | `tag: "around_player"`, and 5 of them have `nodeRef: 0` |

Those five prove the tag alone places the scene: it is not a hint alongside a
node. So:

```python
{'$type': 'scnWorldMarker',
 'nodeRef': {'$type': 'NodeRef', '$storage': 'uint64', '$value': '0'},
 'tag': cname('around_player'), 'type': 'Tag'}
```

**This is what makes "Johnny appears beside V and says one line" a scene rather
than an unvoiceable caption.** A `spawnDespawn` actor's `spawnOffset` is then
relative to V, precisely what a script-side `DynamicEntitySpec` spawn was
doing by hand. Except a scene line carries a RUID and can therefore be voiced.

Use it for any beat whose position is not known in advance. Use a NodeRef when
the beat has a real place (a bar counter, a specific room) and you want the
actor exactly there.

**`around_player` sits on the player exactly, and it carries V's rotation.**
Measured in game, five runs (`backlog.md` §9):

| Offset given | Where the actor landed | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| (0, 0, −2.5) | 0.00 m horizontally from V, 2.50 m down | 2, facings 175° apart |
| (0, 2, 0) | 2.00 m, 0° off dead ahead | 2, facings 180° apart |
| (1.1, 1.8, 0) | 2.10 m, 32° off ahead, on the RIGHT | 1 |
| (−1.1, 2.4, 0) | 2.64 m, 24° off ahead, on the LEFT | 1 |

So the marker is at V's exact x and y, and in `spawnOffset`, **+Y is FORWARD
and +X is RIGHT**, in V's own frame. An offset can be aimed. The recipe is
below.

Line style still turns on ONE field:

| line style | positional? | marker |
|---|---|---|
| `holocall=True` (`isHolocallSpeaker`) | no, 2D through the phone | anything |
| `inner_vo=True` (`Vo_Expression_InnerDialog`) | no, 2D | anything, including nowhere |
| `inner=True` (the same, plus `visualStyle: innerDialog`) | no, 2D, in V's head | `around_player` is fine |
| default (`Vo_Expression_Spoken`) | yes | a real anchor near the speaker |

The 2D rows are the useful discovery, and the first came free: Johnny and Mama
Welles had *identical* setups. Buried actor, Tag marker, and he was fine while
she was not. The only difference between them is `inner`.

**`inner` sets two fields, and only one of them does that work.** Separated in
game 2026-08-17 by routing the two lines of one conversation differently: with
`voExpression` alone, and with both. Both were audible and both subtitles read
the same, so `voExpression` carries the 2D behaviour and `visualStyle:
innerDialog` is styling that changes nothing visible on a speaker who is not
Johnny. Use `inner_vo` for anyone who is not a relic in somebody's head: an
Arasaka fixer standing in front of V should sound un-located without his
subtitle claiming to be a hallucination.

A speaker whose lines are 2D needs no placement at all, which is worth more than
it sounds. The actor can sit at the default offset a kilometre out, where it
cannot be seen, walked into, or watched disappearing when the scene exits. Every
attempt to HIDE a body instead is a guess about the geometry under a marker, and
`gotchas.md` #36 is what one of those cost.

### Putting an actor in front of V, facing him: the whole recipe

Two fields on the actor's `spawnDespawnParams`, and nothing else:

```python
spawnOffset.position     = (aside, ahead, 0)   # metres, in V's frame
spawnOffset.orientation  = yaw_to_face_player((aside, ahead, 0))
```

`aside` is positive to the RIGHT, `ahead` is positive FORWARD, both in metres.
`questkit.scene.yaw_to_face_player` returns the yaw that turns the actor back to
look at V from wherever the offset puts him.

**Never type the yaw by hand.** A fixed 180 is the obvious guess and it is
correct only from dead ahead: at (1.1, 1.8) it left the actor looking 32 degrees
past V's shoulder, which reads on screen as talking to nobody. The helper
returns 180 for the dead-ahead case, so the guess is a special case of it.

What this gives, confirmed in game on the arasaka beat:

- the scene places the actor, so he arrives already posed. **No T-pose.**
- no burial, no script placement, no workspot device, no per-tick targeting query
- he is the line's speaker, so lipsync and the voiceover map still work
- it works anywhere, so a beat whose position is unknown in advance needs no
  special handling

Vanilla's one Tag-marker Johnny scene (`sts_pac_cvi_02_johnny.scene`) gives its
actor its own `spawnMarkerNodeRef` and a zero offset, so there is no vanilla
precedent for offsetting off a Tag marker. That is an absence of precedent
rather than evidence against, and it is worth knowing before you assume the
approach is standard.

#### The exit flash: the scene plays it itself

A scene removes its `spawnDespawn` actors on the frame it exits, so without an
effect the actor POPS out of existence rather than fading. Arrival is fine, it
comes free with the workspot's own materialisation.

The old script route covered this by playing `johnny_teleport_start` on him and
deleting him 0.25 s later, so the vanish happened behind the flash. That
calibration was made by ear across 1.2 / 0.45 / 0.25 s. With the scene owning
the actor there is nothing to delete and no hook to hang a listener on, and a
1.5 s script tick cannot catch a 250 ms window in any case: the old code polled
at 0.15 s to manage it.

**`scneventsVFXEvent` is the answer, and it is a shipped, heavily used class.**
1161 of the 7067 `.scene` files in `basegame_4_gamedata.archive` carry one
(counted 2026-08-17). `questkit.scene.Scene.fire_vfx` emits it and
`stage_johnny` fires one 250 ms before the last section ends, on the same number
as the `cc_g01_johnny_exit` cue, which stays for a future script to use.

Every field is `q101_07c_johnny_triggers.scene`'s, which plays this exact effect
on Johnny twice. Two traps it settles:

- **`effectInstanceId` is `4294967295`, not zero, in both halves.** Zero is a
  real index into the scene's own `effectDefinitions`, so an event written with
  it points at an effect resource the scene does not ship. In that file 27 of 28
  events carry the sentinel; the one that carries `(0, 0)` is the scene's own
  `q101_pills_spill.effect`, declared with `effectId: 0`.
- **A named effect needs no declaration anywhere.** `johnny_teleport_start` is
  not in that scene's `effectDefinitions` at all. Effects named on an event
  resolve against the performer's own entity.

A performer-attached effect leaves `nodeRef` at 0 and names a performer
(`actorId + 1`). A WORLD effect is the mirror image: `performerId` 4294967040
and a real `nodeRef`.

The effect names are shipped and must never be guessed:
`johnny_teleport_start`, `johnny_teleport_end`, `johnny_appear_glitch`,
`johnny_spawn_slow` / `_normal` / `_fast`, `glitch`,
`johnny_glitch_idle_01..03`. A wrong CName does nothing at all and looks
exactly like the bug you are chasing.

#### A scene-placed actor does not sit perfectly on uneven ground

Seen in play 2026-08-17: Johnny clips scenery and his feet sink below the
pavement on a kerb. Harmless for an apparition. It matters because it decides
how any future SOLID NPC gets placed.

The offset is baked at build time and measured from V's own Z, so any difference
between the ground under V and the ground under the actor shows as sinking or
floating. `validateSpawnPostion` does not help: it rejects grossly invalid
spawns rather than fine-placing anyone.

Two honest options for a solid NPC, and they cannot be combined:

| Route | Ground | Pose |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed world anchor (NodeRef), authored on real geometry | exact | correct, no T-pose |
| Script placement with a navmesh query | exact | **T-pose**, see `backlog.md` 9 |
| Scene placement on `around_player` (what ships now) | approximate | correct, no T-pose |

Runtime ground-snapping and scene-owned posing are mutually exclusive: only a
script can query the navmesh, and only a scene can pose the actor on arrival.

**Audio falls off with distance. It is not on/off.** The playtest report of
Mama Welles as *"low and far, almost cannot hear it"* established two things at
once: a mod-registered voiceover map IS consulted for world lines, and the only
variable is distance from the listener.

If a line seems silent, ask whether it is silent or merely quiet before
suspecting the map.

One more caveat: **a scene that never starts stalls the quest phase waiting on
its exit.** A scene that starts and fails to stage an actor merely plays a silent
line. The first is the failure worth guarding against; keep quest-advancing facts
in the script, not in the scene.

### Hub density: the number to aim at

Measured across 188 vanilla scenes:

| | vanilla |
|---|---|
| hubs with exactly ONE option | 560 of 1118 (50%) |
| dialogue sections per hub | 3.8 |
| scenes with no hub at all | 27 of 188 (14%) |

Single-option hubs are idiomatic, so "one option" is not the problem. **Density
is.** A hub after every block of dialogue is four times vanilla's rate and reads
as being interrupted.

The trap that produces it: with no player actor, every V line has to be a hub
option, so the hub count is pinned to V's line count and cannot come down without
cutting dialogue. Give V a section instead. Keep a hub only where he is
deciding something, and then follow it with the section where he says
the line: that is vanilla's shape.

### The button is a PARAPHRASE, not the line

This section used to say the button and the subtitle carrying the same text was
"correct rather than a duplication bug". **That was reasoned, never measured,
and it is wrong.** playtest, 2026-08-13: *"the choice on which I need to press F is
very long because it contains the whole string that is then said by V."*

Measured over 320 vanilla scenes (street stories + side quests), 1376 choice
options:

| | vanilla |
|---|---|
| button text also appears verbatim as a spoken line | 11.1% |
| button length | median 18 chars, 90th pct 37, max 76 |
| spoken line length | median 40 chars, max 255 |
| buttons ≤ 40 chars | 93.6% |

Gig 01's worst offender was 103 characters. Longer than any of the 1376. The
shape vanilla uses is a short paraphrase of the *intent*, then the full line
spoken:

```
'I have an appointment'        -> 'I have an appointment with Ms. Koch'
'Why me?'                      -> "Why me? Isn't it better to hire a detective?"
'I need to talk to someone'    -> "I need to talk to my friend. I've seen him..."
```

**Rule of thumb: keep a button under 40 characters.** The subtitle keeps the
comic wording; the button only has to say which thing V is about to do.

## Do not fake a choice

A hub whose options all lead to the same reply is not a choice, and it reads as
broken - the player weighs two lines, picks one, and the conversation ignores
which. playtesting called this out on the El Coyote scene (2026-08-12), and it was
true of three of the four scenes.

The source comic is linear: V says every one of his lines, in order. So every
hub in this gig now carries exactly ONE option. That is the game's normal "your
turn to speak" beat and it is honest about fixed dialogue - the player is pacing
the conversation, not steering it.

Rule for anything new: **give a hub more than one option only when the options
lead somewhere different.** Different reply, different branch,
different fact - something. Two paths that reconverge on the same next line with
no trace of the decision is worse than no choice at all, because it promises
agency the scene cannot honour.

A useful check, cheap to run after regenerating:

```python
multi = [n['Data']['nodeId']['id'] for n in graph
         if n['Data']['$type'] == 'scnChoiceNode' and len(n['Data']['options']) > 1]
```

Anything it lists should be a deliberate branch you can point at.

## Where the text really comes from (the blank-subtitle trap)

A scene line points at a `scnlocLocstringId` RUID. The scene's own embedded
`locStore` looks like where that resolves. It even holds per-locale text, but
**the game ignores it at runtime.** It is editor data.

The real lookup is a `localizationPersistenceSubtitleEntries` JsonResource whose
`stringId` is the same RUID. Reached through two resources, which is the
part that costs a playtest:

```yaml
localization:
  subtitles:
    en-us: mod\<mod>\localization\subtitles.json    # a MAP, not the text
```

ArchiveXL's `subtitles:` key merges into the base game's
`base\localization\<lang>\subtitles\subtitles.json`, and **that file is a
`localizationPersistenceSubtitleMap`: a list of file paths, not lines.** So ship
two files:

| Resource | Class | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `subtitles.json` | `localizationPersistenceSubtitleMap` | one `localizationPersistenceSubtitleMapEntry` (`subtitleFile` depot path + `subtitleGroup: quest`), registered in the `.archive.xl` |
| `gig01_lines.json` | `localizationPersistenceSubtitleEntries` | the real `stringId` → female/male text table |

Register the *map*. Registering the entries file directly gets you exactly one
line in the ArchiveXL log and nothing anywhere else:

```
[Localization] Resource "mod\...\subtitles.json" failed to load.
[Localization] Subtitles merged with issues.
```

**Check that log after any subtitle change**: it is the only place this
reports. `localizationPersistenceSubtitleEntry` also has separate
`femaleVariant` / `maleVariant`, picked by V's body type: that is how Mama Welles
says "mija" or "mijo" to the right V.

(Deceptious never hit the map, because OneMoreLight ships its entries file at a
base-game path the base map ALREADY references, overriding a vanilla subtitle
file rather than adding one).

`subtitles` is a first-class `.archive.xl` key alongside `onscreens` (both are
in ArchiveXL's string table, targeting `base\localization\<lang>\subtitles\
subtitles.json`). Register it for every language: the content is English-only
and English beats a blank line.

**Symptom when it is missing:** everything works except the words. The scene
runs, choice hubs appear and route correctly, sections advance on schedule, and
every row and every subtitle is empty. Nothing errors; there is nothing in any
log. `tools/gen_scenes.py` therefore emits the scenes and `subtitles.json`
together, and refuses to run if two line keys hash to the same RUID.

Proof, if it is ever needed again: OneMoreLight ships
`base\localization\en-us\subtitles\quest\jackie\jackie_default.json`, and 53 of
its 56 scene locstring RUIDs appear in it as `stringId`s.

### `alwaysCinematicNoSpeaker`: SUPERSEDED 2026-08-13

For a line whose speaker is not present. Nothing in this gig uses it any
more.

**Read this heading before the section below it.** Every speaker in gig 01 is
now a present actor or a player actor, so `regular` and `innerDialog` are what
ship. Playtesting confirmed the standard styling, red name and cyan text, as
"perfect".

`alwaysCinematicNoSpeaker` is the fallback for a speaker who cannot be
staged. Reach for a present speaker first: the styling is the reward for having
one.

The original diagnosis, still accurate about the mechanism:

Distinct from the two failures above, and it looks almost identical from the
player's seat: with the subtitle table finally loading, **choice options read
correctly and nothing the NPC says ever appears.**

Choice options are UI text. Dialogue lines are subtitles, and a `regular` line
needs a speaker `GameObject` the subtitle system can reach. Our speakers are
voice-only actors a kilometre away, so every line resolved its text and was then
dropped.

`scnDialogLineVisualStyle.alwaysCinematicNoSpeaker` (= 7) is the engine's own
style for a line with no speaker present. It is the same lesson as the scripted
route, which needs `scnDialogLineType.OwnerlessRegular` for exactly this reason
(`architecture.md`, "Spoken lines without a .scene"). Cost: no speaker name over
the line. Fine when the call UI already names the caller, or the NPC is standing
in front of the player.

Also: do not set `validateSpawnPostion` on a voice-only actor. The spawn
point is deliberately out of the world, which is precisely what validation
rejects, and an actor that fails to spawn cannot speak.

### Correction to "actors cannot be bound to spawned entities"

The section above says a scene actor cannot be bound to an entity the mod spawns
at runtime. That is still true in that direction, `DynamicEntitySpec` has no
`uniqueName`. It works the other way round instead: a scene actor's
`spawnDespawnParams.dynamicEntityUniqueName` is a real handle, and quest nodes
can address the spawned actor by it through
`gameEntityReference.dynamicEntityUniqueName`. Vanilla does exactly this to put
Johnny into a workspot after spawning him. Let the SCENE spawn, then reference.

### Two routes, and which to use where

**Scene actor**: spawns at the scene's FIXED marker. Gets lipsync, workspot
idles, choice hubs, and full scene staging. Use it wherever V's position is known
in advance: Hoshino's lounge, El Coyote.

**`DynamicEntitySystem` spawn from script**: spawns anywhere, including next to
V wherever V is standing. This is how AMM and friends put him on screen, and it
is what `Gig01_Encounter.reds` already does for Hoshino, the guards and Mama
Welles. Use it for a beat that can happen anywhere - the moment Elena's location
lands is the case in point, because a scene actor would materialise in an Arroyo
car park while V is across the city.

The dynamic route costs lipsync, workspot idles and dialogue choices. What it
keeps is the thing that matters most for a subtitle: **a real GameObject to hang
the line on**. `scnDialogLineData.speaker` is a `wref<GameObject>`
(`orphans.swift:44913`), so setting it plus `scnDialogLineType.Regular` gives the
standard presentation WITH his name, instead of the ownerless styling every
absent speaker is stuck with. Spec fields: `recordID`, `appearanceName`,
`position`, `orientation`, `tags`: `appearanceName` is what carries
`silverhand_default` and therefore the apparition look.

### WHY HE WAS INVISIBLE - solved 2026-08-12

`silverhand_default` is not a "look". It is the appearance whose meshes all use
`meshAppearance: "blendable"`, and **a blendable mesh renders nothing unless
`gamePhantomEntitySystem` is actively driving it.** We were spawning a correct,
fully-present Johnny wearing a material nobody was turning on.

The chain, all from shipped data:

- The mesh's own appearance table maps `default` to an ordinary opaque
  `multilayered.mt`, and `blendable` to `silverhand_overlay_blendable.mt`.
- Counting `meshAppearance` per appearance in `silverhand.app`:
  `silverhand_default` is blendable x15, and so are `riot`, `blendable` and
  `clean_2020`. `silverhand_riot__not_blendable` and
  `silverhand_clean_2020_not_blendable` are default x20 - no blendable at all.
- `entVisibilityParamSource` has exactly ONE member, `PhantomEntitySystem`
  (`orphans.swift:354`). Entity-level visibility overrides exist for the phantom
  system and nothing else, and `gamePhantomEntitySystem` has zero scripted
  methods - there is no way to register with it.
- `phantomVisibleStates` is `["RootMotion","Workspot"]`, and a spawned puppet in
  a plain idle is in neither.
- `blendableAppearanceMatches` is the engine's OWN escape hatch and it names the
  substitute: `silverhand_default` pairs with `silverhand_riot__not_blendable`.
  The mechanism is an appearance swap, not an entity hide - which is why a
  non-blendable appearance works even with the phantom component still attached.
- Vanilla precedent: `q101_07c_johnny_triggers.scene` spawns him through
  `spawnDespawnParams` with exactly `appearance: silverhand_riot__not_blendable`.
  The street-story scenes that use `silverhand_default` are precisely the ones
  that follow with a workspot, i.e. they satisfy the state gate.

**So: spawn him with a blendable-free appearance and he renders - solid, no
dither, no rim glow, no glitch. The apparition look IS the phantom path;** there
is no way to have it without satisfying `phantomVisibleStates`, which in practice
means a workspot.

Blendable-free appearances on `johnny.ent`, i.e. the ones that will render:
`silverhand_riot__not_blendable`, `silverhand_clean_2020_not_blendable`,
`silverhand_default__cyberspace` (stylised - the closest thing to otherworldly
without the phantom system), `silverhand_riot_no_spikes`, `silverhand_wounded`,
`silverhand_wounded_bandaged`, `silverhand__q101_bomb_bag`, `q108_johnny_default`.

**AMM corroborates every step of this, having hit the same wall.** It does not
spawn `Character.Silverhand` at all. It clones Takemura, repoints
`entityTemplatePath` at its own shipped `.ent` with the phantom component
removed, and offers an appearance list that is exclusively blendable-free,
checked against the histogram above. Every blendable appearance is excluded.
That is not a style choice, it is the same bug found and worked around. Its
database even labels the base `Character.johnny_mirror` record as
"Johnny Mirror (Invisible)" - and that template has NO phantom component and
still spawns invisible, which isolates the appearance as the cause rather than
the component.

If a blendable-free appearance somehow still shows nothing, the fallback is
AMM's shape: ship a copy of `johnny.ent` with `gamePhantomEntityComponent`
deleted, and point a TweakXL `Character.cc_g01_johnny` (`$base:
Character.Silverhand`) at it via `entityTemplatePath`. `DynamicEntitySpec` also
takes a `templatePath` directly (`ResRef` literal syntax in redscript is
`r"base\path\file.ent"`), but that route loses the record wiring and with it
the speaker name.

### THE FIX: a scene carries its OWN workspot, no world node needed

The blocker was always assumed to be "a workspot needs a `worldWorkspotNode` in a
streaming sector, and mod-sector nodes do not resolve". **That assumption was
wrong**, and vanilla proves it.

`sts_wat_kab_03_johnny.scene` - a Johnny street-story apparition - carries
`workspots[]` and `workspotInstances[]` in the scene resource itself, and three
of its four instances use:

```
playAtActorLocation: 1     workspotNode: 0     originMarker.nodeRef: 0
```

A workspot played at the actor's current position, with no world node at all.
The node that drives it is a `questUseWorkspotNodeDefinition` with
`scnUseSceneWorkspotParamsV1`, addressing the actor by
`entityReference.dynamicEntityUniqueName` - the handle a scene-spawned actor
already has:

```
function: UseWorkspot     playAtActorLocation: 1     teleport: 1
isWorkspotInfinite: 1     jumpToEntry: 1             entryId: 2
changeWorkspot: 1         enableIdleMode: 1          meshDissolvingEnabled: 1
movementType: Walk
```

(An earlier draft of this block said `entryId: 59`. It is 2: 108 of the 110
workspot nodes across the 27 shipped Johnny scenes use 2, and the only exceptions
are 7 and 10 on walk/sit cycles).

pointing at a `scnWorkspotInstance`, which points at a
`scnWorkspotData_ExternalWorkspotResource` naming an ordinary shipped resource.

**This satisfies `phantomVisibleStates: Workspot`, which means it is the route to
the REAL see-through apparition** - not a solid stand-in. It is what CDPR ships
for exactly this situation.

**BUILT 2026-08-12** in `tools/gen_scenes.py`: `add_workspot_node()` +
`fire_workspot()`. What follows is the part that was not obvious from the
research and cost the most time to get right.

#### Two node variants, and only one is usable

| params type | addresses | usable by a mod |
|---|---|---|
| `questUseWorkspotParamsV1` | a `workspotNode` NodeRef in a streaming sector | no - mod-sector nodes never resolve |
| `scnUseSceneWorkspotParamsV1` | a `workspotInstanceId` inside the scene | yes |

They share a class (`questUseWorkspotNodeDefinition`) and differ only in the
`paramsV1` handle, so a dump that does not print `$type` makes them look
identical. `sts_hey_gle_04_johnny.scene` contains both.

#### Where to hang the node: events socket, not main flow

Vanilla wires these two ways and the choice is a real engineering decision:

- **Main flow** (`sts_hey_gle_04` node 7): `start -> workspot -> "Work Started"
  (osock ordinal 1) -> first section`. Guarantees the workspot has begun before a
  word is said. Stalls the scene indefinitely if it never starts - and a
  scene that never reaches its exit point is a quest phase waiting on that exit
  for the rest of the playthrough.
- **Events socket** (`sts_wat_kab_03`, all four of its `playAtActorLocation`
  nodes): the section carries a `scneventsSocket` event that fires output socket
  **name 2** at a given `startTime`, and the workspot node hangs off it with
  nothing after it. Fire and forget.

**This repo takes the events socket.** If the workspot fails we are back to
exactly the previous behaviour - an invisible Johnny whose lines still play -
instead of a gig that cannot be finished. Given that no published mod has ever
made this work, failing back to the known state is worth more than guaranteeing
the ordering.

Fire it at `startTime` 0 of the scene's first section, not of Johnny's own
section. He is meant to be standing there throughout; materialising for his own
line is worse than not being there.

#### Sockets, which are all positional and all silent when wrong

- `isockMappings` is `["CutDestination", "In"]`, so linking INTO a quest node
  means destination ordinal 1. Ordinal 0 wires its cutscene-skip path.
- `osockMappings` is `["Success", "Work Started"]` - ordinal 0 and 1 of output
  socket name 0.
- Section output sockets are written in `(name, ordinal)` order: `(0,0)` flow,
  `(1,0)` empty, `(2,0)` the events socket. `Scene.validate()` now refuses a node
  whose sockets are out of order, because appending the name-2 socket before the
  name-1 one does not error - it just quietly does nothing.

#### Picking the .workspot resource

Surveyed all 27 shipped Johnny scenes (110 workspot nodes). Requirements for a
free-standing commentator: standing, needs no prop, and **proven with
`playAtActorLocation: 1`** somewhere in shipped data - a resource only ever used
against a world node is not evidence it works without one. Three survive:

- `base\workspots\main_characters\johnny\johnny__stand_ground__stand_around__02.workspot`
  - chosen. His own rig, plainest standing idle, palc=1 in `mq023_01_johnny`.
- `...\sq031\sq031_01a_smack_afterlife\sq031__johnny__stand__wait_nervous__01.workspot`
  - the most-used palc=1 standing Johnny in the game (7 nodes), but a fidgety idle.
- `base\workspots\common\ground\generic__stand_ground__think__01.workspot`
  - what `sts_hey_gle_04_johnny.scene` itself uses. Generic. Best fallback.

Resource refs use `Flags: "Default"`, not `Soft`: a workspot that has not
streamed in is an animation that does not play.

#### Do NOT copy vanilla's interruptionScenarios here

The research note below says to copy `scnCheckSpeakersDistanceInterruptCondition
{ Greater, 6 }` so the scene pauses if V walks off. **That would break every
scene in this gig.** Our non-Johnny speakers are voice-only actors parked at the
marker plus (1000, 1000, -100) - a kilometre out. A "speakers further apart than
6 m" condition is true the instant the scene starts. It stays unshipped until the
voice-only-actor design goes away.

### The script placement route: BUILT, SHIPPED, AND DELETED 2026-08-17

Everything below this heading used to be the recipe. It is kept in one short
section because the findings inside it are reusable and because a route that was
shipped for months should not vanish without a record, but **do not build any of
it**. The placement recipe above is the whole answer now.

What the route was, and why it existed: a scene actor's `spawnOffset` was
believed to be unaimable, so the scene could not put an actor beside V. The
workaround was to split him in two. The scene owned the words and buried its
actor 2.5 m under the floor; the script found a second body with a 40 m
targeting query, placed it with an invisible workspot device, and played arrival
and exit effects to cover the handover.

It rested on a claim that was never measured and turned out to be false. The
marker sits at V's exact position and carries V's rotation, so the offset is
perfectly aimable. `backlog.md` 9 has the measurements and `gotchas.md` #31 has
the lesson. Deleting the workaround deleted, in one change: the T-pose two
players reported (it was the bind pose flashing as the body moved between two
workspots), the mid-air arrivals, a 40 m query running 6.6 times a second that
could grab another mod's Silverhand, 645 lines of redscript, the workspot entity
and its generator.

**Findings from it that still apply**, because they are about the engine rather
than about the route:

- **`TeleportationFacility.Teleport` ignores the position for a puppet** and
  drops him on the player. Measured: asked for a spot 210 cm from V, he landed
  210 cm from it. This is why the route needed a workspot device at all.
- **The workspot is not decoration, and the scene still fires one.**
  `gamePhantomEntityComponent.phantomVisibleStates` is
  `["RootMotion", "Workspot"]`, so a Johnny in a plain idle is invisible AND
  untargetable. `stage_johnny` fires the workspot at t=0 of the first section
  for this reason; see the workspot sections above.
- **`WorkspotGameSystem`'s real method names**, all compiler-confirmed, since
  the plausible-sounding ones (`PlayInWorkspot`, `StopInWorkspot`,
  `SendFastForwardSignal`, `SendJumpToAnimEnter`) do not exist:
  `PlayInDeviceSimple`, `StopInDevice`, `StopNpcInWorkspot`,
  `IsActorInWorkspot`, `SendJumpToAnimEnt`.
- **Three traps in authoring a `.ent` by hand**, each of which cost a failed
  conversion. `components` entries are inline objects and NOT
  `{HandleId, Data}` handles, which is the opposite of `entity` and of scene
  events, and WolvenKit rejects the file with a bare `JsonException` naming only
  a line number. `entity` must be `gameObject` and not `entEntity`, or the cast
  every script does returns null. And `compiledData` must be omitted entirely,
  because WolvenKit regenerates it from `components`.
- **`DynamicEntitySpec.templatePath` is ignored when `recordID` is set**, so a
  spawned NPC is either a record with its own template or a template with no
  record and reduced capability. `gotchas.md` #30.
- **A synchronous resource reference on a dynamically spawned entity hard
  crashes the game.** `gotchas.md` #29.

### Root motion is NOT a script route

No script function plays a named body animation on a puppet. `PlayAnimation` and
`gameanimPlayAnimEvent` do not exist. `AnimationControllerComponent`'s statics
only push values into the existing anim graph and cannot be shown to reach a
root-motion state. `MoveOnSpline` needs an authored spline NodeRef. So of the
three `phantomVisibleStates`, Workspot is the only one a mod can reach.

### If the phantom component has to go instead

AMM's `silverhand.ent` has 87 components and no phantom component, and its
`compiledData` is a fully-formed buffer consistent with that list - i.e. it was
REBUILT in a tool, not hand-patched. Hand-deleting a component from the JSON
`components` array while leaving `compiledData` describing the original list
**crashes the game on load** (learned 2026-08-12). If a modified `.ent` is ever
needed, build it properly; do not edit the array by hand.

### The risk to plan for

`gamePhantomEntityComponent.phantomVisibleStates` is `["RootMotion", "Workspot"]`.
Read literally, the phantom renders only in those states, so a Johnny standing
in a plain idle could be invisible. This could not be verified: the system is
native, has no scripted API, and nothing in the decompiled tree touches it.

The circumstantial evidence is consistent though: every vanilla scene that
spawns him via `spawnDespawn` immediately follows with a
`questUseWorkspotNodeDefinition` (`teleport: 1`, `isWorkspotInfinite: 1`), and
the two that do not acquire him from world placements that already carry a
workspot.

**Treat "he spawns but cannot be seen" as the most likely first playtest
failure.** Plan the workspot as the fix, not as polish.

The workspots vanilla uses are `worldWorkspotNode`s in the street story's own
streaming sector. Whether a mod-authored one resolves as a NodeRef is untested,
and mod-sector marker nodes are already known to fail for map pins.

Two other things to copy from vanilla:

- an `interruptionScenarios` entry with
  `scnCheckSpeakersDistanceInterruptCondition { Greater, 6 }`, so the scene
  pauses if V walks away. This repo emits an empty list. That was right for a
  speaker a kilometre off and is wrong for one standing next to V.
- a `questCameraFocus_ConditionType { inverted: 1 }` wait, so he never visibly
  pops out on camera.

### What the existing mods do, and why they are not the route

Neither gives you the apparition.

AMM spawns a solid, opaque, posable body with one bark and no dialogue. Grepping
its spawn code for hologram, transparent, ghost or glitch returns nothing. Its
notes record that Johnny was excluded from companion mode for years, and that
some `silverhand_*` appearances visibly deform on a spawned copy. Here's Johnny (Deceptious) is a real lipsynced scene
performance, but explicitly reuses existing vanilla voice lines and options text,
and ships no source. No public mod renders a see-through Johnny. The vanilla
data route above is better than either.

## Audio: a scene line is voiced through a VOICEOVER MAP

**Corrected 2026-08-12. This section previously concluded the opposite**: that
because Audioware cannot voice a scene line, a voiced scene must have its lines
driven from script alongside it. The premise was right, the conclusion was wrong:
it never considered the path the game itself uses. Full evidence in
`backlog.md` 2a; the mechanism, briefly:

A line's `scnlocLocstringId.ruid` is the same number the game uses to find
both the subtitle text *and* the audio. Subtitle text comes from a
`localizationPersistenceSubtitleEntries` resource keyed by that RUID (see the
module docstring in `gen_scenes.py`). Audio comes from a `locVoiceoverMap` - 
`base\localization\en-us\voiceovermap.json` and its `_holocall` / `_helmet` /
`_rewinded` siblings, which is a flat list of
`locVoLineEntry { stringId, femaleResPath, maleResPath }` with **no scene, quest
or actor scoping whatsoever**.

ArchiveXL merges mod entries into it: `localization:` accepts `onscreens`,
`subtitles`, `lipmaps` and `vomaps`. So the recipe is the same shape as the
subtitle one already in use. Ship a `.wem` per line, ship a vomap pointing our
RUIDs at them, declare it in the `.archive.xl`.

Two consequences:

- **Reusing a vanilla line costs nothing but a number.** Point the line's
  `locstringId` at a vanilla `stringId` and emit no subtitle entry for it;
  vanilla's own registration supplies text and audio, gender-correct, with
  nothing shipped. Three lines in gig 01 do this (`backlog.md` 2c).
- **`customVoEvent` is still a dead end.** `scnDialogLineVoParams` has one, but
  resolving it needs a real soundbank. Do not emit them.

**AUDIOWARE IS NOT USED AND IS NOT A DEPENDENCY** (settled 2026-08-13). This
paragraph used to reserve it for the scripted-caption beats, since nothing in the
scene system can voice a caption. True, and the answer was to stop having
captions. All of them were rebuilt as scenes, so the mod ships no Audioware
requirement and players install nothing extra.

Section timings come from the real clip, not the ~55 ms/char estimate:
`gen_voice.py` measures every WAV into `durations.json` and `gen_scenes.py` reads
it. Run them in that order: the other way round times the scene to the
previous run's audio.

## Lipsync: the mouth is a fifth thing keyed by the same RUID

**CONFIRMED IN GAME 2026-08-14**: *"perfectly lip synced"*, on a scene actor,
using an animation borrowed from an unrelated vanilla line and matched only by
LENGTH. Read `tools/gen_lipsync.py`'s docstring for the full mechanism; this is
the part that belongs with scene authoring.

**It lands on the line's SPEAKER.** That is the whole difficulty for this repo,
not the data: wherever the visible body is not the scene's own actor, the mouth
that moves is one nobody can see.

A line moves a mouth through four pieces, and a mod supplies the last two:

| # | piece | note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `base\localization\en-us\lipsync\<scene depot path>\<actor>.anims` | one set per actor per scene, animations named `f_<stringId hex>` |
| 2 | `base\localization\en-us.lipmap` | `animLipsyncMapping`, merged into by ArchiveXL's `localization: lipmaps:` |
| 3 | `resouresReferences.lipsyncAnimSets[]` | indexed by `scnActorDef.lipsyncAnimSet.id` |
| 4 | `scnscreenplayDialogLine.female/maleLipsyncAnimationName` | a CName naming one animation in that set |

Three things that are not guessable:

- **The lipmap's three arrays are PARALLEL**: `scenePaths[i]`,
  `scenePreviewPaths[i]`, `sceneEntries[i]`; and inside an entry,
  `actorVoiceTags[j]` ↔ `animSets[j]`. `scenePaths[i]` is **FNV1a64 of the
  scene's depot path**. Verified against all 3495 vanilla entries.
- **Vanilla's piece 3 points at files that ship in no archive.** So give every
  speaking actor a real `voicetagId`: the lipmap is keyed by it, and `0` cannot
  be looked up.
- **The animation NAME is free-form.** Nothing has to be baked: point a line at
  an animation that already exists in a shipped set, and the mod ships no
  animation data at all. Pick by LENGTH: that is what `gen_lipsync.py` does.

Set both `female...` and `male...` to the same `f_` name. The prefix is the
PLAYER's body type, not the speaker's, and an NPC line is one recording:
`sts_hey_gle_04_johnny.scene` asks for an `m_` animation that does not exist in
Johnny's set.

## STAGING A CHARACTER WHO SPEAKS, LIPSYNCS AND STANDS BESIDE V

**One call does all of it: `Scene.stage_johnny(first_section, last_section)`.**
Confirmed in game 2026-08-17 across all seven of gig 01's Johnny beats.

The name is historical. Nothing in it is specific to Johnny except the default
actor, and it is the pattern to copy for any character who has to appear next to
the player, face them, talk, and leave.

It does three things, and each one is above in this file:

| | how |
|---|---|
| where he stands, and which way he faces | `spawnOffset.position` + `yaw_to_face_player`, on an `around_player` marker |
| that he can be seen at all | a workspot fired at t=0 of the FIRST section |
| that he does not pop when he goes | `scneventsVFXEvent` playing `johnny_teleport_start` on the LAST section, 250 ms before it ends |

**This used to be a six-step split between a scene and a script**, with the
speaker buried under the floor and a second body lifted into place. That is
gone; see "The script placement route" above for what it was and which of its
findings survive. What follows is what is still true about scene-owned actors,
all of it independent of how they are placed.

### The workspot is load-bearing, and it must fire on the FIRST section

`gamePhantomEntityComponent.phantomVisibleStates` is
`["RootMotion", "Workspot"]`, so an actor in a plain idle is invisible AND
untargetable. Fire it late and he is missing for the part of the scene before
it. Reference the first section by name, never by a variable that used to mean
"first": passing the wrong section here is silent, because firing an events
socket on a valid section is valid whichever section it is.

### WHERE IT APPLIES, AND WHERE IT CANNOT

**A scene actor exists only while its scene runs.** That decides everything:

| the character... | pattern? |
|---|---|
| exists only for his own lines (an apparition) | yes |
| must be present across several scenes, or between them | yes, but one staging EACH - he glitches out and back in at the gaps |
| must exist before or after the scene (found, talked to, killed) | no - nothing can be there to speak |

In gig 01 every Johnny beat takes it. Hoshino cannot - V has to find him and
shoot him, both outside the scene. Mama Welles cannot - she is a base-game
NPC standing in a bar. Those two keep a buried voice-only speaker and no visible
lipsync, and it is a property of the beat, not a bug.

### A SCENE ACTOR CANNOT EXIST BEFORE ITS OWN SCENE STARTS

Which decides where lines live. If a line needs him already on screen, that line
belongs in HIS scene - V's "Got it. Wait. That's-" moved out of Elena's call for
exactly this. If a beat must happen after something the scene itself ends
(a holocall only hangs up when its scene EXITS), it needs its own scene: that is
why Johnny's p30 pair is `gig01_graves` and not the tail of the Nix call.

Moving a line between scenes changes its RUID. **Copy the `.wem` to the new key
and md5-verify it**, move the key in `gen_voice.CAST`, and never regenerate a
take to move it. `gen_voice` refuses to build if the two tables drift.

### `OnlyLipsync`: moving a mouth that is not the speaker's

`scnAdditionalSpeakerRole` has two members, `Full` and `OnlyLipsync`, and
`scnDialogLineEvent.additionalSpeakers` names extra actorIds. `OnlyLipsync`
gives a second actor the mouth movement and nothing else, no audio, no
subtitle, no name.

**This is vanilla's answer to exactly the split this repo lives with.**
`q000_kid_01b_meet_your_fixer` and `q115_03a_alt` speak their lines as the
PLAYER actor and hang the lipsync on `v_male_tpp` / `v_female_tpp`, the
third-person doubles you can see.

**Vanilla's doubles, though, ALWAYS EXIST. They are `spawnDespawn` actors.** An
earlier draft of this section read the two of them being listed together as
proof that an absent additional speaker is tolerated. It proves nothing of the
kind, and gig 01 crashed on 2026-08-14 three seconds into the first scene
that named a double which could fail to acquire. There is no shipped example of
an additional speaker that is not there. If a double can fail, assume the line
event will dereference it.

So: keep the buried voice-only actor as the speaker (audio, subtitle and name
are already right), and add a second actor that is only there to be looked at.
Failure is quiet: no double means no lipsync and nothing else.

### Acquiring the visible body, and the one thing still untested

`scnEntityAcquisitionPlan` has nine members. Which one a double uses is the
whole question, because the body has to already exist:

- **`spawnSet`**: how vanilla acquires a base-game NPC standing in the world.
  `mama_welles_default.scene` uses entry `mama_welles` + reference
  `#mama_welles`, and it is copied verbatim for gig 01's epilogue. If a
  base-game NPC has an ordinary conversation scene, read its acquisition rather
  than inventing one.
- **`findInWorld` + `actorRef.type = Tag`**: the only handle a
  `DynamicEntitySpec`-spawned entity has, since that spec has no `uniqueName`.
  `gameEntityReferenceType.Tag` and `gameEntityReference.names` are real, and
  **no shipped scene uses them**: all 251 `findInWorld` actors across 7067
  scenes are `EntityRef` with a NodeRef.

**TESTED IN GIG 01 ON 2026-08-14. IT DOES NOT WORK, AND IT CRASHES. Settled - 
do not retry it without new evidence.** The script's `DynamicEntitySystem` tag
was verifiably populated at the moment the scene ran
(`IsPopulated(n"cc_g01_johnny")` true, logged four seconds before the scene) and
the scene did not bind that body. **The scene's `findInWorld` resolver does not
read the dynamic-entity tag registry.** Separately, the `additionalSpeakers`
entry naming the unbound actor crashes the game at scene TEARDOWN - 
deterministically, 4.3 s after scene entry in both runs, to a tenth of a second.
So a scene actor can only be acquired by something that resolves: a NodeRef, a
spawn set, a community entry.

**The general lesson, which is bigger than lipsync: an enum member is not a
feature.** Both of the inferences that produced this crash were "vanilla has a
field for it, so this must work". Before relying on a value, grep the shipped
scenes for a USE of it, `convert serialize` a corpus and count, the way the
`around_player` marker and the hub-density numbers in this file were settled.
If the count is zero, treat it as unimplemented until a playtest says otherwise,
and put it where its failure is cheap: **a scene that never reaches its exit
point strands the quest phase, and one that crashes ends the session.**

A scene actor cannot be bound to an entity the mod spawns at runtime:

- **`DynamicEntitySpec` has no `uniqueName` field.** Probed with the redscript
  compiler on 2026-08-11; the fields are recordID, templatePath, appearanceName,
  position, orientation, persistState, persistSpawn, alwaysSpawned, spawnInView,
  active, tags. The `dynamicEntityUniqueName` a scene uses is the scene system's
  own naming for actors *it* spawned.
- A base-game community NPC (Mama Welles) has no NodeRef discoverable offline.

So every non-player speaker in this repo is an `acquisitionPlan: spawnDespawn`
actor with a `specRecordId`, spawned for its voice only.

**WHERE it spawns was a bug that outlived this note** (fixed 2026-08-13). It used
to be the scene marker plus `(1000, 1000, -100)`: a kilometre out and a hundred
metres down, copied from Californication's trick for Judy. That offset was
invented for SUBTITLES, which do not care where a speaker is. **Audio does, and
it attenuates rather than switching off**, so every positional line in the gig
was inaudible while presenting as a routing failure. Positional speakers are now
buried ~2.5 m under the listener, or under a fixed anchor near their mark; only
the holocall speakers still sit far away, because 2D audio does not care.

The body the player actually sees
is still the one the encounter script spawns or finds; the scene supplies the
words, the choices, and the name over the subtitle (which the subtitle
controller takes from the speaker's Character record `displayName`).

That last point is why each speaker needs a TweakXL Character record at all - 
`source/tweaks/elena.yaml` exists purely so a name appears above her lines.

**Do not invent a `$base` record.** TweakDBIDs are case-sensitive and a wrong one
fails silently; base a new speaker on an id this project has already verified.

### Facial expressions on a puppet

Cheap, and the numbers are the game's own rather than guesses. Push an
`AnimFeature_FacialReaction` through
`AnimationControllerComponent.ApplyFeatureToReplicate`. The mapping is published
by `ReactionComponent.SelectFacialEmotion`: disgust is category 3, idle 7;
aggressive is category 3, idle 1.

Treat it as best effort. If the anim graph ignores the feature on a workspotted
puppet, the actor keeps a neutral face and nothing else breaks, so this is safe
to attempt and not worth building a fallback for. Gig 01 does not set one on
anybody, which is why the shipped scenes all play deadpan.

## Wiring a scene into the quest phase

`questSceneNodeDefinition` (`tools/gen_questphase.py: add_scene`):

- `sceneFile`: depot path of the `.scene`. Nothing needs declaring in the
  `.archive.xl`; the reference mods do not declare theirs either.
- `sceneLocation`: a `scnWorldMarker` NodeRef that resolves, same rule as
  map pins. It is only a placement origin, not a trigger: the player does not
  have to be anywhere near it, precisely why a fixed anchor works for a
  phone call. Reuse the pin anchors.
- **Sockets are named after the scene's entry and exit points**, plus the fixed
  `CutDestination`, `Default INT`, `Default RET`, `Prefetch`. A wrong name does
  not error. The graph just never continues.

### Where to put the logic

Vanilla puts fact-setting and waiting *inside* the scene, as `scnQuestNode`s
wrapping real quest node definitions. We deliberately do not: every branch here
ends at its own exit point and the quest phase does the fact work, so there is
one graph builder to maintain instead of two. Revisit only if something needs to
happen part-way through a section: that is what `scneventsSocket` is for.

## Validating offline

There is no substitute for playing it, but two checks catch most of it:

1. `WolvenKit.CLI convert deserialize <scene>.json`: a bad enum value or a
   flat object where a handle belongs fails here. (Scene events must be
   `{HandleId, Data}` handles, not inline objects).
2. Round-trip: deserialize, then `convert serialize` the result and diff against
   the source. Anything the JSON layer silently dropped or defaulted shows up.
   A clean round-trip differs only in TweakDBIDs it cannot reverse-hash (mod
   records) and float/int formatting.

`tools/check-scripts.ps1` is also an API oracle: put a probe `.reds` in the
tree and a wrong field or method name becomes a compile error instead of a
silent no-op in game. That is how `DynamicEntitySpec.uniqueName` was ruled out.
